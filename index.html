About Our Clinic

A local small business dedicated to your holistic health journey

Meet Patrick & Jodie Stewart

Patrick and Jodie Stewart are licensed massage therapists who met while studying massage therapy at Carrington College before beginning to practice professionally in 2019. Together they founded Rebalance Point Massage with a shared commitment to providing thoughtful, evidence-informed therapeutic bodywork.

Their work is grounded in a science-based approach to massage therapy and draws from techniques such as Muscle Energy Techniques (MET), myofascial therapy, trigger point therapy, and lymphatic drainage to address pain, improve mobility, and support the body's natural recovery processes.

Their sessions are often highly customized to each client's short- and long-term goals, addressing specific musculoskeletal complaints and functional restrictions while taking into account the broader structural and compensatory patterns that may be contributing to them.

Their clinical experience includes working with acute musculoskeletal issues and chronic pain patterns, particularly involving the spine, shoulders, and pelvic complex, helping clients restore mobility, reduce discomfort, and improve functional movement.

At their Spokane clinic, Patrick and Jodie aim to provide a professional and welcoming environment where clients can receive individualized therapeutic care tailored to their needs and long-term wellbeing.

"Massage is where art and science meet through the medium of touch to create healing"

Research & Benefits

Selected research on the benefits of therapeutic massage

Stress Reduction

Massage therapy may help reduce perceived stress and promote relaxation. Proposed hormonal and neurochemical mechanisms remain uncertain.

International Journal of Neuroscience
View Research

Pain Management

Massage therapy may reduce pain and improve function in some musculoskeletal conditions, with evidence varying by condition and treatment approach.

"Massage therapy may be effective for treatment of chronic back pain."

Annals of Internal Medicine
View Research

Sleep and Massage

Some studies have reported sleep improvements after massage, but findings are limited and appear to vary across populations.

"only the massage therapy group reported an increase in the number of sleep hours."

Journal of Clinical Rheumatology
View Research

Immune-Related Biomarkers

Preliminary research has found short-term changes in some immune-related biomarkers following massage, but the clinical significance of these findings remains uncertain.

Journal of Alternative and Complementary Medicine
View Research

Blood Pressure

Repeated massage has been associated with short-term reductions in blood pressure in some people with elevated blood pressure.

"mean systolic and diastolic blood pressure in the massage group was significantly lower."

Iranian Journal of Nursing and Midwifery Research
View Research

Mental Health

Research suggests massage may help reduce measured symptoms of anxiety and depression in some populations.

"Reductions of trait anxiety and depression were MT's largest effects."

Psychological Bulletin
View Research

Research has examined massage as a complement to conventional care for specific conditions and outcomes.
